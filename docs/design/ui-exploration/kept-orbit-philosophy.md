# Kept Orbit — a design philosophy

*A movement of held revolution: independent panels circling one incontrovertible core.*

**Kept Orbit** is the aesthetics of custody. Its central form is always the same
gesture: a small, dense, saturated sphere — singular, unarguable — wrapped by a
broken cylinder of curved panels that revolve around it without ever touching it or
each other. The panels are staves; the composition is a hull mid-build, a vessel
held in dock. Nothing is sealed. The gaps between panels are not absences but the
movement's signature: every opening is an honest sightline to the core, proof that
the center survives inspection from any angle. Work in this movement must read as
*held*, never frozen — the eye should complete the rotation the panels only begin.

Form is cylindrical and interrupted. Panels are segments of a true ellipse-projected
band — mathematically exact arcs, never freehand — with varied heights and vertical
offsets, like planks trimmed by different hands to the same jig. Some pass behind
the core, some in front; this interleaving is the movement's only permitted illusion
of depth, and it must be constructed by occlusion order alone, never by blur or
shadow theatrics. One soft ground-shadow may anchor the mass. Everything else floats
on generous, disciplined emptiness: the orbit owns at least as much silent margin as
it does painted surface.

Color is a working harbor at dusk. Deep keel navy carries the structure; a
practical, community green does the labor; one stave of signal yellow keeps watch;
clear water blue closes the ring. The core alone is red — a single saturated organ,
lit once from the upper left, gradient-turned like lacquered wood. Panels may carry
the faintest lengthwise gloss, as if freshly varnished, but the movement survives
being flattened to pure flats: if the mark fails in one ink, it was never crafted
well enough. No color repeats the core's red anywhere in orbit. Ever.

Scale and rhythm follow the stave rule: no two adjacent panels share a height, a
width, or a vertical seat, yet all obey the same two ellipses — the discipline is
invisible until measured, and measuring it should feel like discovering the jig in a
master boatwright's loft. Typography, when it must exist, is clinical and whispered:
a thin mono for coordinates and captions, a single confident wordmark set close to
the form, small figures noting angles and radii the way an observatory logs
transits. Text annotates the orbit; it never competes with it.

Every artifact of Kept Orbit must look labored over — the product of countless
hours, painstaking attention, master-level execution. Arc endpoints meet their
ellipses exactly; gaps are tuned in degrees, not eyeballed; gradients turn on the
same light source to the half-degree. The finished piece should feel like the one
surviving plate from an imaginary discipline that studied how a truth is kept:
patient, systematic, meticulously crafted, and quietly certain that what circles the
center — however many hands made the panels — is one graph, one record, one core.
